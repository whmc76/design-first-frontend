# Design-First Visual Production

Turn a video or graphic-design request into a coherent production system, not one attractive frame. The design contract covers creative direction, deliverable families, typography, subtitles, motion, audio cues, real content/assets, format adaptation, implementation feasibility, and rendered-output verification.

## Outcome Standard

A successful result must be:

- **Systematic:** the program, series, campaign, or channel has recognizable rules rather than unrelated assets.
- **Coverage-complete:** every scoped deliverable family, format, language, timeline moment, and materially different application has approved evidence or an explicit scope decision.
- **Content-true:** styleframes use real or representative titles, names, durations, footage conditions, logos, and legal copy without inventing unavailable rights or capabilities.
- **Readable:** titles, subtitles, lower thirds, credits, data, and calls to action survive representative imagery, motion, compression, and viewing conditions.
- **Motion-literate:** timing, transition, reveal, interruption, continuity, and reduced/quiet alternatives serve communication rather than decoration.
- **Production-feasible:** the design maps to real source assets, editing/compositing tools, templates, render/export settings, platform constraints, and an affordable workflow.
- **Verified:** authoritative stills, sequences, templates, and final renders are inspected at their actual formats—not approved from a moodboard or isolated hero frame.

## Medium Boundary

- Use this skill for non-interactive video identity, motion packages, editorial graphics, static campaign systems, and cross-format visual production.
- Use `$design-first-frontend` for interactive routes, application shells, browser state, responsive panels, and real UI controls.
- For mixed products, keep one shared brand source of truth: this skill owns media/timeline/delivery rules, while `$design-first-frontend` owns interactive behavior and browser verification.
- Use a dedicated image-retouching skill for bounded pixel repair where the creative system and delivery package are already settled.

## Non-Negotiables

- Inspect the real brief, brand/product context, content structure, source assets, intended platforms, deliverable list, languages, and production pipeline before proposing polish.
- Create a creative coverage matrix before generating styleframes. The matrix—not the first generated image—decides how many artifacts are required.
- One styleframe is valid only for an explicitly single-output scope with no distinct timeline, format, subtitle, or application decision left unresolved.
- Separate accessible captions from decorative typography and editorial supers. They may share visual DNA but have different readability, timing, localization, and delivery obligations.
- Do not use a contact sheet, moodboard, or montage as the sole authority for type size, spacing, safe areas, motion, timing, or export fidelity.
- Preserve product truth and rights. Do not invent logos, footage, music, talent likenesses, licenses, claims, platform capabilities, or client approvals.
- Freeze a creative, production, and delivery contract before broad asset production. If the design depends on uncertain animation, compositing, localization, or template behavior, create a narrow motion/production proof first.
- Never claim a video is complete from still frames, or a graphic package is complete from one master composition. Verify actual outputs and adaptations.

## Choose the Right Evidence

- **Creative coverage matrix:** deliverable families, formats, languages, states/timeline moments, template families, and governing artifacts.
- **Visual system board:** logo/lockup behavior, typography, color, material, image treatment, graphic motifs, iconography, and density.
- **Master styleframes:** authoritative compositions for each distinct deliverable/template family.
- **Storyboard, motion board, or animatic:** timing, sequence, transitions, continuity, edit rhythm, and audio relationships.
- **Type/subtitle stress frames:** representative light/dark/busy footage, long names, multilingual copy, two speakers, dense captions, credits, and small-screen viewing.
- **Executable proof:** template controls, responsive/adaptive layout, expressions, alpha, localization, data-driven text, or uncertain render behavior.
- **Rendered sample:** representative final export with compression, motion, sound, captions, and platform framing when those are in scope.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish production truth

- Identify the audience, message, channel/program/campaign job, brand position, tone, and what must remain recognizable.
- Inventory scoped outputs: video package elements, graphic families, aspect ratios, durations, languages, caption modes, print/digital use, and platform placements.
- Inspect real logos, fonts, footage, stills, music/audio, copy, names, data, legal requirements, licenses, templates, and existing production files.
- Record the actual editing, compositing, design, captioning, handoff, and render pipeline. Do not assume a specific application.
- State the observed problem and first bad boundary: brief, content system, identity, hierarchy, timeline grammar, type/subtitle system, template model, asset rights, export contract, or rendered output.

### 2. Map coverage before polish

- Read [references/creative-coverage.md](references/creative-coverage.md).
- Build the deliverable/application graph and collapse it into meaningful coverage units instead of generating every size or timeline permutation.
- Assign stable coverage and artifact IDs. Record which master/template governs each adaptation.
- Stop if the requested scope, source assets, platforms, languages, or required outputs remain materially unknown.

### 3. Define the creative system

- Choose the identity strategy: editorial, cinematic, broadcast, creator-led, documentary, entertainment, education, product, institutional, or another justified model.
- Define the recognizable system: logo/lockups, type roles, palette, contrast model, image/footage treatment, compositional motif, information density, and signature transition or graphic behavior.
- Define what remains invariant across video and static applications, and what may adapt by format, hierarchy, or viewing distance.
- Prefer a small set of strong rules with broad application over decorative effects that cannot survive content changes.

### 4. Pass the production-feasibility gate

- Map each visible/textual/audio value to a real source, rights status, owner, update path, and fallback.
- Map each deliverable to its production method, template owner, editable parameters, render/export path, and acceptance evidence.
- Build a narrow proof when motion, tracking, keying, alpha, multilingual type, automatic resizing, data-driven text, or cross-application handoff is uncertain.
- Use real extreme content and representative footage conditions in the proof.

### 5. Freeze the production contract

- Read [references/production-contract.md](references/production-contract.md).
- When on-screen text, subtitles, captions, lower thirds, credits, or localization matter, read [references/subtitles-and-type.md](references/subtitles-and-type.md).
- For title sequences, bumpers, transitions, animated packaging, audio cues, or template motion, read [references/motion-packaging.md](references/motion-packaging.md).
- Produce or select the artifact set required by the coverage matrix. Use separate full-resolution masters and keep stable artifact IDs.
- Freeze the creative system, coverage mapping, content/type rules, timeline grammar, motion/audio rules, adaptation logic, asset rights, template behavior, delivery matrix, and explicit exclusions.

### 6. Produce in coherent families

- Establish the system board and the highest-leverage master styleframe before multiplying adaptations.
- Produce dependent video and graphic artifacts in small coherent families, preserving shared type, color, motif, image treatment, motion grammar, and naming.
- Use master templates for repeated applications, but do not force structurally different deliverables into one brittle template.
- Keep text and media editable when handoff requires iteration. Preserve source provenance and avoid flattening away required controls.
- Render representative extremes early: longest title/name, busiest background, smallest placement, alternate language, failure/missing-media case, and critical transition.

### 7. Verify actual outputs

- Read [references/verification.md](references/verification.md).
- Verify every coverage unit against its governing artifact and delivery requirement.
- Inspect full-resolution stills at intended size and representative small previews.
- Inspect actual motion exports at normal speed, around cuts/transitions, and under representative compression.
- Verify captions/subtitles for timing, line breaking, contrast, safe placement, language/script support, speaker/SDH treatment, and delivery mode.
- Confirm filenames, dimensions, duration, frame rate, color/alpha behavior, audio/caption outputs, print/export requirements, and editable source handoff against the real specification.

### 8. Run the quality challenge

- **Audience:** Is the program/message recognizable and readable without explanation?
- **Editor/producer:** Can the system handle new episodes, names, footage, languages, durations, and platform variants without redesigning everything?
- **Designer:** Is the identity distinctive and coherent, or mainly fashionable effects applied to unrelated layouts?
- **Motion designer:** Do timing, rhythm, hierarchy, and audio relationships explain the sequence?
- **Accessibility/localization:** Do captions, contrast, scripts, text expansion, and quiet/reduced alternatives remain usable?
- **Skeptic:** Which five real content or delivery conditions would break the package first?

Continue when an answer exposes a coverage, legibility, continuity, feasibility, or delivery failure.

## Root-Cause Pattern for Revisions

Before repairing a reported mismatch, state:

- Observed symptom.
- First bad boundary.
- Suspected root cause.
- Why it surfaced in this asset, timeline moment, language, format, or export.
- Whether the proposed change fixes the system/template/contract or only patches one output.

Frequent root causes include a single hero frame standing in for a package, undefined content hierarchy, captions treated as decoration, no busy-footage test, one layout stretched across incompatible ratios, motion invented after static approval, missing rights/source assets, contradictory masters, and export settings never tested on the target platform.

## Completion Standard

The final report must include:

- Creative brief and approved system source.
- Coverage totals by deliverable family, format, language, timeline/state, master/template, and artifact ID.
- Master styleframes, storyboards/motion proofs, subtitle/type evidence, and representative final outputs required by scope.
- Production implementation, editable sources/templates, asset provenance/rights status, and real delivery matrix.
- Verification evidence for representative content extremes, actual renders/exports, adaptations, captions, motion/audio, and platform/print behavior.
- Explicit deviations and reasons.
- Zero unexplained, `PLANNED`, or `BLOCKED` coverage rows; otherwise report the result as incomplete with the exact blocker and owner.

Do not say “complete,” “broadcast-ready,” “platform-ready,” or “production-ready” when only concepts, still styleframes, or unverified exports exist.
