# Motion Packaging

Use this reference for title sequences, program openers, stings, bumpers, transitions, lower-third animation, kinetic type, bugs, end cards, loops, and coordinated audio cues.

## Motion Grammar

Define a small reusable vocabulary rather than animating every element differently:

- Primary reveal and exit behavior.
- Secondary text/data behavior.
- Transition family and edit/cut relationship.
- Depth, masking, wipes, transforms, type animation, footage treatment, or other signature mechanism.
- Hold states, loops, idle behavior, and how repeated use avoids fatigue.
- Static/quiet alternative and behavior when audio is absent.

Tie each motion rule to hierarchy, continuity, orientation, emphasis, or brand recognition. Remove motion whose only purpose is to appear premium.

## Timeline Contract

```markdown
| Element | Timeline trigger | Communication purpose | Entry/hold/exit | Properties | Duration/easing class | Cut/footage relation | Audio cue/sync | Loop/interruption | Static/quiet result | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
```

Use durations and easing derived from the actual editorial rhythm, platform, and program identity. Do not impose universal timing constants across openers, captions, live graphics, short-form clips, and end cards.

## Audio Relationship

- Define whether music, sound design, voice, or silence drives the cue.
- Record sync points, tails, edit handles, ducking/mix ownership, and what happens when the asset is shortened or extended.
- Use only licensed or authorized audio. A temporary cue must be labeled and must not silently enter final delivery.
- Verify a no-audio or quiet version when the placement may autoplay muted or accessibility/context requires it.
- Follow the actual loudness, peak, channel, and delivery specification rather than inventing defaults.

## Production Proof

Create the smallest rendered proof that establishes:

- Real timing and readability, not motion implied by arrows on a still.
- Behavior over representative footage and around actual edits.
- Entry, hold, exit, repeated use, interruption, and last-frame state.
- Alpha/matte/compositing behavior when overlays are delivered separately.
- Template controls, text extremes, duration changes, localization, and failure/missing-media behavior when automated.
- Render performance and dependency/plugin availability proportionate to the pipeline.

## Motion Gate

Do not freeze motion from prose alone when timing materially affects the result. Approve a storyboard/animatic for sequence logic and a rendered proof for the riskiest motion, compositing, type, audio, or template invariant.
