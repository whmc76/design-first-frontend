# Visual Production Verification

Verify the actual still, sequence, template, and delivery files—not only the design board or editor preview.

## 1. Preflight

- Confirm coverage ID, artifact ID, source project/version, linked assets/fonts/plugins, content version, language, platform/placement, and delivery row.
- Confirm the authoritative brand, copy, logo, legal, caption, footage, audio, and rights sources.
- Rule out stale caches, offline proxies, missing fonts, substituted media, disabled effects, wrong color management, or preview-quality renders.

## 2. Static and Graphic Outputs

- Inspect the layered/editable master and the exported output.
- Verify dimensions/aspect, crop/focal point, bleed/safe region when applicable, resolution, color/profile, transparency, compression, and file format against the real delivery matrix.
- Check hierarchy and legibility at intended display size, realistic viewing distance, and representative small preview.
- Exercise longest/shortest copy, bright/dark/busy imagery, missing media, alternate language, and constrained adaptation.
- Compare families side by side for logo, type, palette, motif, image treatment, spacing, and content-priority continuity.

## 3. Video and Motion Outputs

- Watch the complete representative render at normal speed with sound, muted when relevant, and around every transition/cut boundary.
- Inspect entry, hold, exit, first/last frame, repeated use, loops, interruption, edit handles, and shortened/extended variants when supported.
- Verify text remains readable during motion and does not depend on a paused hero frame.
- Check dropped/duplicated/black/flash frames, unintended clipping, aliasing, banding, stutter, alpha/matte edges, compositing, media relink, and compression artifacts proportionate to the deliverable.
- Verify duration, frame rate, dimensions/aspect, codec/container, color/alpha, audio channels/mix, caption tracks/sidecars, and file naming against the delivery matrix.

## 4. Subtitles and On-Screen Type

- Check transcription/translation accuracy against the approved source and actual audio.
- Check sync, segmentation, line breaks, exposure, speaker/SDH treatment, punctuation, language/script rendering, and sidecar/burn-in behavior.
- Inspect over representative light/dark/busy footage, near faces/key action, with lower thirds/bugs/CTAs, and under platform overlays.
- Verify accessible captions are not weakened by decorative motion, low contrast, tiny text, or collision with packaging.
- Test localization/text expansion without silent truncation or arbitrary shrinking.

## 5. Package Continuity

- Verify each coverage row against its governing master/artifact and output proof.
- Confirm adaptations inherit the right system rather than drifting through manual recreation.
- Confirm different families remain recognizably related without forcing incompatible layouts into one template.
- Verify asset provenance, rights/credit/legal requirements, editable source handoff, and dependency availability.
- Confirm obsolete or rejected masters/exports cannot be mistaken for current sources of truth.

## 6. False Positives

- Editor preview looked correct but final export substituted fonts, effects, proxies, color, or media.
- Hero frame passed while entry/exit, long names, busy footage, or vertical adaptation failed.
- Contact sheet looked coherent only because individual assets were too small to inspect.
- Captions looked readable on a clean test background but failed on representative footage or platform UI.
- One language passed while another clipped, reversed, lost glyphs, or required a different master.
- Master file worked on the creator's machine but depended on missing fonts, plugins, links, or unlicensed assets.
- File specifications were guessed rather than verified against the current delivery destination.

## 7. Final Challenge

Answer with evidence:

- Does every scoped deliverable family and material adaptation have an approved governing artifact and verified output?
- Can a producer/editor update real content without breaking hierarchy, timing, captions, or brand continuity?
- Do the smallest, busiest, longest, fastest, multilingual, and most compressed representative cases remain usable?
- Are accessible captions and legal information correct, readable, and delivered in the required mode?
- Are motion and audio doing communicative work, and do muted/static alternatives remain coherent?
- Are editable sources, rights, dependencies, naming, and delivery specifications complete enough for the real owner to continue production?

If any answer is weak, continue or report the exact incomplete state, blocker, owner, and affected coverage rows.
